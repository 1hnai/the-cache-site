Viberg has been making boots in Victoria, British Columbia since 1931. The Service Boot is the style that put them on the map for anyone paying attention to quality footwear — a clean, uncomplicated silhouette with construction that genuinely rewards long ownership.

The upper is Natural Chromexcel from Horween Tannery in Chicago, leather that gets more interesting with age. The tanning process is proprietary and involves a hot-stuffing technique that drives oils and waxes deep into the hide. The result is leather that develops a characteristic pull-up effect, where creases lighten and pressure marks darken, creating a patina that's entirely personal to how you wear them.

Underneath, a single-piece midsole and a Dainite rubber outsole — hard-wearing, resoleable, good in light rain without being heavy. The last is Viberg's 2030, which gives a slightly tapered toe that keeps the boot looking intentional rather than clunky.

The construction is Goodyear welt, which matters because it means the boots can be resoled when the time comes rather than thrown away. Buy once, have them resoled every few years, and you're looking at footwear that outlasts most of what else you own.

At £545 they're a significant spend. They're also one of the clearer examples of a thing that costs more upfront and less over time.
