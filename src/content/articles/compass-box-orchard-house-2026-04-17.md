Compass Box occupies an unusual position in Scotch: the company buys casks from distilleries across Scotland, blends them to a specific flavour profile, and bottles the result with more transparency than most. Orchard House is their accessible expression — intended to be the bottle you reach for regularly rather than save for an occasion.

The name is accurate. First-fill American oak contributes vanilla and coconut alongside the fruit, and the Clynelish malt they use as a base brings a waxy, orchard-ripe quality that runs throughout. There's enough Speyside sweetness to make it unchallenging on first encounter, but the blend holds interest across a glass — the finish has a light spice that arrives late and lingers.

It works with a single large ice cube if the evening is warm, or neat if you're drinking slowly. The 43% ABV is calibrated to be expressive without demanding dilution. This is the kind of whisky that makes sense to keep on hand because it fits almost any context: dinner accompaniment, late drink, gift.

Compass Box publishes the recipe and cask provenance for most of their expressions, which feels unusual in an industry that treats blending recipes as trade secrets. There's something refreshing about knowing what you're drinking and why it tastes the way it does.

At £45, Orchard House is one of the more honest bottles in its price bracket — flavourful, consistent, and made with visible intention.
