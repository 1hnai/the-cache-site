Instant cameras have been coasting on novelty for years. The Instax Mini 99 is a different proposition — it actually treats you like someone who cares about the shot.

The headline feature is six colour effect modes built into the lens mechanism: natural, warm, cool, soft, vignette, and light leak. These aren't filters applied in software. They're produced optically at point of capture, which means the print you pull from the camera is the finished article. No editing, no second-guessing.

Beyond the effects, Fujifilm has added manual exposure compensation (five stops either way) and a dedicated bulb mode for long exposures — genuinely useful for low-light shooting with a tripod. The metal shutter button and textured grip are small things, but they change how the camera feels in the hand. Less toy, more tool.

At £139 it's a meaningful step up from the basic Mini 12, but the gap in experience is substantial. Instax Mini film is widely available and costs around £10-12 for ten shots — factor that into your budget if you're shooting often.

It comes in two colourways: dark bronze and dark bronze. There's only one colourway, and it's the right choice.
