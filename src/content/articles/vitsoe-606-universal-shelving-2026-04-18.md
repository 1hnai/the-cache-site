Dieter Rams designed the 606 Universal Shelving System for Vitsœ in 1960. It has been in continuous production ever since, essentially unchanged. That's not inertia - it's a design so resolved that improvement hasn't been possible.

The system uses a wall-mounted aluminium rail and a series of shelves, cabinets, and surface modules that clip into it without tools. You can reconfigure, extend, or subtract at any point. Move house, take it with you. The shelving that fits a London flat rearranges into a configuration that works in a Bristol terrace. Nothing is wasted.

Everything is made in England by craftspeople who have worked on the same system for years. The anodised aluminium rails are cut, the shelves are formed, the cabinets are assembled - all with tolerances that make the system feel like precision engineering rather than furniture.

Rams' ten principles of good design might as well be illustrated by the 606. Useful, understandable, honest, long-lasting, unobtrusive. The system disappears once it's in your space. You see the books and the objects on it, not the shelving itself.

The price - starting around £450 for a basic configuration and rising steeply from there - makes this a considered purchase. But the calculation is different from most furniture. The 606 outlasts the buildings it inhabits. Buy it once, properly.
