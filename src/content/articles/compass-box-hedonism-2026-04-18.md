John Glaser founded Compass Box in 2000 with a specific ambition: to treat Scotch whisky with the seriousness and craft that the wine world applied to its best blends. Hedonism was the first whisky he released, and it remains the one that defines what the company is trying to do.

It is a vatted grain whisky — a category that most of the industry treats as the low-cost filler in blended Scotch, present for volume and economy rather than character. Glaser's argument, made through Hedonism, is that old grain whisky is genuinely delicious in its own right. The expression draws on whiskies from Cameronbridge and other lowland grain distilleries, typically aged 15 to 30 years, and the result has the silky, coconut-cream texture that well-aged grain develops in oak.

The flavour profile is immediate and distinctive: vanilla, coconut, fresh oak, a little butterscotch, and a finish that is longer and more complex than the sweetness on the nose suggests. It is accessible in the sense that it is not challenging, but complexity accrues with attention. There is more happening than the first sip indicates.

Compass Box operates with a transparency unusual in Scotch whisky — they publish component ages and distillery identities where the SWA regulations permit, and where they don't, they have challenged those regulations publicly. Hedonism benefits from that ethos. You are not buying a mystery.

At £185 it is a considered purchase, not a casual one. But it occupies a position in the market that nothing else fills in quite the same way.
