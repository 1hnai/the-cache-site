Filson's Original Briefcase has been in production since 1897. The silhouette is unchanged. What was built to carry survey equipment into the Yukon now handles laptops, notebooks, and the everyday accumulation of documents with equal indifference to the decades piling up around it.

The body is heavy-duty tin cloth — a waxed cotton canvas developed for outdoor work. Water beads off it. It doesn't soak through. The corners are reinforced with Horween bridle leather, the same tannery that supplies the best shoemakers in the business. The hardware is solid brass.

This is not a bag with a colour season. The tan wax and brown leather will darken and patina as it absorbs use. By year five it looks better than it did on the shelf. By year ten it's a different object entirely — worn into the shape of your habits.

One main compartment, two front pockets, a trolley pass-through at the back. The shoulder strap is wide enough to carry all day. It will outlast multiple laptops and at least one job change.

Buy it once. That's the point.
