The chore coat is a French workwear garment with a clear brief: cover work clothes, resist wear, and not impede movement. Corridor NYC's version in indigo selvedge denim honours the brief and adds the dimension of a fabric that improves with age.

The cut is what separates this from both the vintage originals and most contemporary interpretations. Corridor works with a fuller chest and slightly dropped shoulder that allows layering without the stiff, boxy quality that afflicts many chore coats. The length hits mid-thigh — longer than a standard chore coat, short enough that it reads as a jacket rather than a light coat. The collar is a small point that sits cleanly whether worn open or fastened.

The denim is 12oz Japanese selvedge, sourced from Okayama. At this weight it has structure when new — the jacket holds its shape without feeling rigid — and it softens and fades with use in the way that only this type of denim does. The indigo fades where the fabric flexes: elbows, cuffs, collar, and the front placket. After a year it looks like something that was made specifically for the person wearing it.

Hardware is copper rivets and a single chest pocket with a button closure. Corridor makes everything in New York, which means the construction is visible: the seam allowances, the bar tacking at stress points, the clean finish inside. It is a jacket that will outlast most of what you own and get better the whole time.
