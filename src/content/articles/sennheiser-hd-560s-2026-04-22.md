Open-back headphones are a different proposition to closed. There's no isolation — you hear the room and the room hears you — but in exchange you get a sound stage that sounds nothing like listening through cans. It sounds like speakers, except the speakers are inside your head in the best possible way.

The HD 560S is Sennheiser's entry into proper open-back territory. The 38-ohm impedance means it drives well from a phone or a laptop without a dedicated amplifier, which is the first practical hurdle most open-back headphones fail. The frequency response is flat enough to be genuinely neutral — what the recording engineer intended, not a consumer V-curve.

The velour ear pads are comfortable over long sessions. The cable is detachable and uses a standard 3.5mm locking connector. The build quality feels exactly what you'd expect from a German manufacturer who has been making headphones since the 1960s: not flashy, entirely solid.

Use these at home, at a desk, for serious listening. For commuting they're useless — that's what closed-backs are for. But for an evening with a well-recorded record and nothing else to do, there's no better £149 you can spend on audio.
