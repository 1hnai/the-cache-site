The Lamy Safari has been in continuous production since 1980 and remains one of the most sensible objects in stationery. It's made in Germany from ABS plastic, filled with standard cartridges or a converter, and designed to be used hard without ceremony. The charcoal colourway is the correct one — matte, understated, gets on with it.

What makes the Safari worth choosing over cheaper fountain pens is the nib. It writes immediately on contact, skips only when asked to, and the medium nib is balanced well enough that you don't need to develop a particular hold to get consistent results. Lamy has been manufacturing nibs in Heidelberg since 1930. That institutional knowledge is present in a £29 pen.

The triangular grip is an acquired taste. Some people find it prescriptive; others find it the reason they can write for an hour without their hand stiffening. After a week or two it stops registering either way.

The case for a fountain pen over a ballpoint comes down to what writing costs you physically. Fountain pens require almost no pressure — the ink flows with gravity assist rather than being pressed into the page. If you write a lot by hand, that reduction in grip tension accumulates. The Safari is the lowest-friction way to find out if that matters to you. If you don't get on with it, you're out £29 and some cartridge ink. If you do, it will outlast every plastic ballpoint you've ever owned.

Refill with the standard T10 cartridges or the Z28 converter to use bottled ink. The nib is replaceable and upgradeable.
