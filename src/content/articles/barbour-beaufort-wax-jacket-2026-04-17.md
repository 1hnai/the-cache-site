The Beaufort has been in production since 1980, which tells you something. In an industry that cycles through materials and silhouettes constantly, this jacket has remained essentially unchanged because it doesn't need improving.

It's made from 6oz waxed cotton — heavier than a lot of what passes for weather-resistant now — and it's genuinely waterproof in the way that matters: sustained rain, long country walks, days where the weather doesn't let up. The wax ages visibly over time, building a patina that synthetic jackets never develop. When it starts to look dry and lose its sheen, you re-wax it with Barbour's own tin and the cycle continues. This jacket can last decades.

The cut is a mid-length shooting jacket: enough length to cover the lower back when leaning forward, a corduroy collar that softens against the neck, and an unusually generous pocket arrangement — two hand-warming pockets, two lower bellows pockets, two internal pockets and a map pocket inside the right chest. There's room for what you actually need when you're out.

The navy colourway is the most versatile — dark enough to be urban, understated enough to be rural. It sits well over jumpers in autumn and under nothing in mild spring weather. The kind of jacket you reach for repeatedly until you stop noticing you're reaching for it.
