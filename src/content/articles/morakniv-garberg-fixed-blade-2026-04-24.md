Mora has been making knives in Dalarna since 1891. The Garberg is the most serious thing they've ever built, and it still costs less than a round of drinks at most London bars.

The distinction here is full-tang construction — the blade steel runs the full length of the handle rather than terminating partway through. It's a structural detail that separates bushcraft knives from tools, because a full tang won't work loose after a season of batoning and heavy use. The 4.3-inch blade is ground from 12C27 Sandvik stainless, a Swedish steel specification that holds an edge well and resists corrosion better than most carbon alternatives.

The handle is high-friction polymer over a steel skeleton — comfortable in a gloved hand, grippy when wet, and completely indifferent to abuse. The supplied Kydex sheath has a Multi-Mount system allowing belt carry, MOLLE attachment, or lashing directly to kit, which is thoughtful for something at this price point.

At £95, the Garberg sits in an awkward position: too serious to ignore, too affordable to hesitate over. Whether it's a dedicated camp knife or a last-resort tool rattling around the bottom of a pack, it earns its place with zero drama and complete reliability.

It is, simply, excellent.
