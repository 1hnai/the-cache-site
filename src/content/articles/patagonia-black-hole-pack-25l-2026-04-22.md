The Black Hole name refers to the pack's main compartment — one large opening, no complicated organisation, just a deep bucket that takes whatever you put into it. That directness is the point.

The fabric is 100% recycled ripstop nylon with a DWR finish. It has survived years of documented abuse in the Patagonia community: dropped in mud, dragged through overhead bins, used as emergency waterproofing in downpours. The seams hold. The zips run smooth. Nothing about this bag feels like it's saving cost.

At 25 litres it sits at the most useful volume for urban carry with occasional overnight capacity. Big enough for a 16-inch laptop (in the padded sleeve), a change of clothes, and food. Small enough to fit under a seat. The hip belt packs away when you don't need it. The sternum strap adjusts in thirty seconds.

Patagonia guarantees it unconditionally. If anything fails — zipper, buckle, stitching — they repair or replace it. That warranty is worth factoring into the price.

Available in black and a rotating set of colours each season. The black disappears into any context. The colour versions are usually better than they look in photos.
