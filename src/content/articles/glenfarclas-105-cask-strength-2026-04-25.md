The name comes from British proof: 105° proof is 60% ABV. Glenfarclas has been bottling at this strength since 1968, which makes it one of the earliest commercial cask strength releases in Scotland and one of the few that has remained a permanent, year-round expression rather than a limited run or collector's item.

The Grant family have owned Glenfarclas since 1865 and are now on their sixth generation of management. They operate their own floor maltings, fire their stills with direct gas flame (unusual — most distilleries use steam coils), and mature everything in sherry casks. The 105 is a no-age-statement vatting of malt from those casks, bottled without chill-filtration or colouring.

At 60%, it needs water. Add a third volume and the nose opens into dried fruit, Christmas cake, leather, and a specific kind of sweet smoke. The palate is full and round — sherry is the dominant note, but there is enough complexity from the direct-fired spirit to keep it interesting throughout. It does not fade quickly in the glass.

For the price, this is one of the best value cask strength whiskies you can reliably buy. The expression has not been repositioned or reformulated since it launched; the bottle design has barely changed. In an industry that increasingly chases scarcity and collector premiums, Glenfarclas 105 remains exactly what it has always been.
