The premise of AirTag is simple: a small disc you attach to something you want to find, that leverages every iPhone in existence as an anonymous relay point. When you mark something as lost, the network reports its location without the owner of any relay device knowing they participated. It is a genuinely clever use of distributed infrastructure.

The practical result is that keys, bags, wallets, and bicycles become locatable to within roughly half a metre using Ultra Wideband precision — a combination of proximity beeping and a directional arrow that walks you directly to the object. For anyone who regularly loses things, this shifts a chronic minor stress into a five-second problem.

The 4-pack makes sense for most households: one for keys, one for a bag, one for a wallet or luggage, and one spare. Each AirTag runs on a standard CR2032 battery that Apple claims lasts over a year. They are IP67 water resistant, and the tracking history is encrypted so Apple cannot see it.

There is no subscription. There is no monthly fee. The tracking network exists whether or not you think about it. At £99 for four, the maths works out to roughly £25 per tracked item — less than the cost of replacing most of the things you are likely to lose.
