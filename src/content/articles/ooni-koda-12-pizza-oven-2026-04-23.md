The first time you use an Ooni Koda 12, the result is slightly alarming. A pizza that you've been making in a conventional oven for years — and considered perfectly good — is suddenly revealed as the compromise it always was. The Koda runs at temperatures no domestic oven can approach, and it shows.

The design is elegantly minimal: a gas burner, a baking stone, and a folding chimney that doubles as a carry handle. There are no electronics, no timers, no complexity. You connect a standard Campingaz cartridge or a propane bottle with the right adaptor, turn the dial, and wait fifteen minutes. Then you cook.

A twelve-inch pizza takes sixty seconds at full temperature. You rotate it once or twice with a peel to catch the far edge. The crust blisters and chars in the way that marks good Neapolitan pizza — leoparding, in the trade — and the base is cooked through rather than soggy. Toppings heat almost instantly from below and above simultaneously.

At £299 it's a meaningful purchase, but it's also a machine that genuinely earns its space in a garden. It weighs 9.25kg and folds flat for storage. Come summer, it becomes the reason people are coming over.

The 12-inch size handles one pizza at a time, which is actually fine — the cook time is short enough to keep a table supplied. If you regularly feed eight people, the Koda 16 exists.
