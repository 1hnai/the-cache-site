The original Anglepoise lamp was designed in 1933 by automotive engineer George Carwardine, who applied the mechanics of the human arm — constant-tension springs, balanced joints — to create something that could hold any position without drift. The Type 75 Mini is the direct descendant of that thinking, scaled down and refined for the modern desk.

What makes it worth £175 when a generic adjustable lamp costs £25? Precision of movement. The spring tension is calibrated so the arm stays exactly where you put it. There's no gradual sag, no drift toward the horizontal when you want it pointing at your keyboard. It just holds. After a decade of use, it will still hold.

The shade pivots independently of the arm, giving you fine control over the beam direction. Available in a range of considered colours — from matt black to warm terracotta — it looks good in the box before it even comes out. Made in Britain, covered by a two-year warranty, and designed to be repaired rather than replaced.

Some objects are just correct. This is one of them.
