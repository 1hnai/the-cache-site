Joseph Opinel designed this knife in 1890 in the Savoie region of France and essentially got it right on the first attempt. The No.8 has a 85mm blade, a beech wood handle, and a Virobloc rotating collar that locks the blade open or closed. That's the entire design. It has not meaningfully changed since.

The carbon steel version is the one to own. It takes a sharper edge than stainless and holds it longer — the trade-off is that it patinates with use, developing a dark oxide layer that actually protects the blade and tells the story of where it's been. A weekend camping trip will mark it permanently. This is a feature.

Weigh it in your hand and it feels almost impossibly light. Carry it in a jacket pocket and you'll forget it's there until you need it. The blade deploys with a satisfying click, locks solid, and unlocks with a quarter turn of the collar.

At fifteen pounds it's the best value cutting tool in existence. It belongs in every kitchen drawer, rucksack, and jacket pocket. Buy several — you'll leave them places.
