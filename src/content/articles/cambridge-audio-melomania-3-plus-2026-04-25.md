Cambridge Audio built their reputation in the 1970s making amplifiers that sounded considerably better than they had any right to for the money. The Melomania 3+ earbuds carry the same idea into wireless audio: at under £100, they deliver a sound quality that consistently surprises people expecting budget-tier results.

The driver is a single 6mm dynamic unit with a composite diaphragm — no hybrid driver arrangement, just one well-engineered transducer tuned carefully. The tuning is what Cambridge Audio tends to do right: balanced with a slight lift in the bass that never becomes boomy, and enough detail in the upper frequencies to resolve texture in acoustic instruments and vocals. They are not the last word in spatial imaging, but they are honest in a way that many earbuds at two or three times the price are not.

Practicalities: 9 hours per charge from the buds themselves, another 27 from the case. IPX5 rated, so rain and sweat do not require consideration. The fit is secure without the pressure that some in-ear monitors create over long sessions. Bluetooth 5.2 with aptX Adaptive when your source supports it, which noticeably tightens the gap with wired audio.

The Melomania range sits at an awkward price point for Cambridge Audio's competitors — too cheap to ignore as a bargain, too good to dismiss as a compromise. The 3+ is the current version; the incremental improvements over the original are real, mostly in noise isolation and call quality. For everyday listening these are the earbuds to recommend.
