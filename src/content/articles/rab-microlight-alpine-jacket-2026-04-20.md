Rab makes insulation for people who use it seriously, and the Microlight Alpine is the jacket in their range that covers the widest range of conditions for the lowest weight penalty. It packs to roughly the size of a water bottle and weighs 300 grams for the men's medium. That matters if you're carrying it as a belay layer or emergency insulation on a day out that turns cold.

The fill is 900-fill Nikwax Hydrophobic Down — treated to shed moisture rather than clump when it gets damp. This is meaningfully different from standard down at altitude or in shoulder-season conditions where you're generating heat on the way up and standing still on a ridge. The jacket won't insulate indefinitely in sustained rain, but it recovers faster than untreated down when you're back under shelter.

The construction is box-wall baffled with a Pertex Quantum outer — light, wind-resistant, and durable enough for technical use without the stiff hand that heavier shells produce. There are two hand pockets and an internal chest pocket that doubles as a stuff sack. The hood is helmet-compatible.

At £210 this competes with the Arc'teryx Cerium SL and the Patagonia Down Sweater at the top of the lightweight down category. Rab's advantage is the hydrophobic treatment and the Pertex shell combination — both are better specified for active mountain use than the lifestyle-adjacent positioning of some competitors. If you spend time on hills in variable weather, this is the jacket to carry.
