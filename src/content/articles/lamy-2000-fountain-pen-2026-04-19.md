Designed by Gerd A. Müller in 1966 and largely unchanged since, the LAMY 2000 is one of those objects that makes you wonder why we bother iterating. The brief was simple: a pen for the next hundred years. Fifty years later, it holds up.

The body is Makrolon fibreglass — light, warm to the touch, and finished with a brushed steel clip that's become one of the most copied details in pen design. The hooded nib keeps the steel tine covered when not in use, reducing dry-out and giving the profile a clean, tapered line. The piston fill mechanism draws ink directly from a bottle, skipping cartridges entirely. You fill it, it lasts, you fill it again.

Writing feel depends on the nib size you choose. Medium is the everyday workhorse — smooth without being slippery, with enough feedback to feel engaged. The F nib is better for smaller handwriting or tighter notebooks. Either way, the LAMY nib tolerances are consistent in a way that mass-produced pens rarely manage.

At £195 this sits in the upper middle of the serious fountain pen market. It's not a collector's piece or a status object — it's a working tool that happens to be beautiful. Buy it once, use it forever.
