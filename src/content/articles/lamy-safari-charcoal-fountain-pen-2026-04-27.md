The Lamy Safari is the pen that usually ends the argument about whether fountain pens are worth the bother. It writes reliably from the first stroke, takes standard cartridges with no fuss, and the triangular grip section means your fingers naturally find the right hold without thinking about it. The ABS plastic body is light and robust — this is a pen you can throw in a bag without worrying about it.

The charcoal colourway is the one to get. The matte finish ages gracefully, the clip is understated, and the overall effect is close to pens that cost five times as much. The nib writes smoothly straight out of the box; a fine nib is a good default for most handwriting.

At £21 it removes every reasonable objection to trying a fountain pen. If you've been meaning to make the switch from ballpoints — for journalling, note-taking, or just the pleasure of handwriting that doesn't feel like dragging a felt-tip across sandpaper — the Safari is where to start.
