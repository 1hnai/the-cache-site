The Braun watch range emerged from the same design culture that produced the T3 radio, the SK4 record player, and every object Dieter Rams made at Braun between 1955 and 1995. The principle was always the same: form follows function, remove everything that doesn't serve the purpose, make it last. The BN0035 applies that ethos to a wristwatch.

The dial is the argument. Black background, white indices, a typeface that reads at speed without needing a second look. There is no date window because a date window would require a different position for the six o'clock index and Rams would not have permitted the compromise. This is considered a design choice, not an omission.

The case is stainless steel at 40mm — large enough to read easily, small enough to wear under a shirt cuff. The domed mineral crystal is correct for the period aesthetic. The quartz movement is Swiss-regulated, accurate enough that you'll never think about it.

What the BN0035 does well is occupy the difficult space between serious watch and fashion accessory without collapsing into either. It's not a tool watch — it won't survive depth or impact. It's not a dress watch — the proportions are too practical and the strap too simple. It's a designed object that happens to tell the time, from a company that thought carefully about what a designed object should be.

At £195 it sits in interesting territory. You're paying for the lineage as much as the specification, but the lineage is genuine, and the result is a watch you'll stop noticing is on your wrist in the best possible way.
