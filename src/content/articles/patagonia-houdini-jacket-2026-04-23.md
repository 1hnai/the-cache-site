The Houdini Jacket has been in the Patagonia range for long enough to have become a reference point. It's the jacket other ultralight windshells are measured against, and most come up short on some dimension.

The case for it is simple: 113 grams in a men's medium, compresses into its own chest pocket to fist size, and made from 100% recycled 1.4oz ripstop nylon with a DWR finish that handles wind, a bit of drizzle, and the kind of sharp spring mornings that punish optimism. It's not waterproof — nobody who understands the product thinks it is — but it blocks wind completely and buys you time in light rain in a way that a fleece simply doesn't.

The Houdini is about compromise management. It's lighter than a softshell, more protective than a baselayer, small enough to forget it's in a pack until you need it, and durable enough to survive years of that treatment. The cut is athletic enough to wear cycling or running without looking like a tent.

Patagonia uses Fair Trade Certified factories for this jacket, and the bluesign-approved fabric means the supply chain has been audited. At £120, there are cheaper windshells — Decathlon makes a reasonable one for £30 — but the Houdini's packability and fabric quality hold up across seasons in a way cheaper versions don't.

The black version is the one to buy. It goes with everything and shows no wear.
