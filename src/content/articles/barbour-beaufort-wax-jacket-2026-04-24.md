Some garments earn their reputation over decades of quiet service. The Barbour Beaufort is one of them. First produced in the 1960s, it remains structurally unchanged in the ways that matter: 6oz sylkoil-waxed cotton, a corduroy collar, brass snap fastenings, and enough pockets to make cargo trousers feel redundant. The internal map pocket alone is a design decision that modern technical outerwear still hasn't bettered.

What separates the Beaufort from a costume piece is its relentless functionality. The cut is generous enough for layering without drowning you — important when April in Britain can mean frost at dawn and shirtsleeves by noon. The hem sits low, the sleeves are cut for movement, and the wax finish sheds rain without the synthetic crinkle of most waterproofs.

The case for buying second-hand is strong: a worn-in Beaufort with a fresh re-wax looks more interesting than a new one. But buying new means you get to put in the work yourself. Within two seasons of regular wear, the wax takes on a patina particular to you — it creases at the elbows, softens at the collar, and begins to look properly earned.

Barbour still offers a re-waxing service for life. In an era of disposable outerwear, that warranty says everything.
