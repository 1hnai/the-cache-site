There's a particular pleasure in a tool that refuses to be more than it needs to be. The Fujifilm X-M5 is the smallest and lightest APS-C mirrorless camera Fujifilm has made, and it's better for it.

The sensor is the same 26.1MP X-Trans CMOS 4 unit found in the X-S20 — genuinely excellent, capable of punching well above its weight in low light. But the headline addition is 6.2K video recording, which makes the X-M5 a serious consideration for anyone who shoots both stills and moving image and doesn't want to carry two bodies.

What makes it compelling rather than merely competent is the film simulation engine. Fujifilm's colour science has always been the argument for choosing their system, and the X-M5 gives you all of it: Velvia, Provia, Classic Chrome, Eterna, Acros. Shooting in JPEG and getting a usable image straight from camera is something that still isn't common enough among digital cameras.

The flip-out screen and subject-tracking autofocus are there for the content-creator crowd, but the camera works just as well if you ignore all that and use it as a compact street shooter. It's light enough to take everywhere, which is the only specification that really matters.

At £699 body only, it sits in a market where the competition is either cheaper and more compromised, or heavier and more expensive. The X-M5 occupies useful ground.
