The Field Notes Original Kraft is the object that launched Aaron Draplin's notebook brand in 2007, and it has not changed much since. The covers are 80lb Kraft stock, the inside pages are 60lb white paper with a grid or rule, and the whole thing measures 3.5 by 5.5 inches — the size that fits a back pocket without folding.

That consistency is the point. Field Notes sells limited edition covers and seasonal variations, but the Original remains unchanged because it was right the first time. The binding is saddle-stitched with a metal staple, which is more durable than glue for something that lives in a pocket through temperature changes and occasional rain. The paper takes pencil and most ballpoints without bleed-through. Fountain pens require restraint; Microns work fine.

The Kraft version is intentionally undesigned — no logo on the front cover beyond a small wordmark, a colour palette of tan and black, and typography that borrows from the ledgers and field notebooks used by American surveyors and foresters a century ago. Draplin has written about this at length: the idea was a working notebook, not a luxury object. The price has tracked that intention; at around £11 for a three-pack, these remain genuinely cheap enough to write in without hesitation.

There is a whole category of expensive notebooks that encourage careful use and nice handwriting. Field Notes encourage something better: using the thing until it is full, then starting a new one.
