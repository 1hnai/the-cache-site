Gerd A. Müller designed the Lamy 2000 in 1966. It has not been revised since. The name referred to the far future; the design outlasted the century it was meant to represent.

The body is Makrolon, a fibreglass-reinforced polycarbonate with a faint grain that reads as matte black from a distance and reveals texture up close. The steel nib is hooded, flush with the barrel, with just enough tip exposed to write. The filling mechanism is a piston integral to the body, holding about 1.5ml of ink. The clip is spring steel, tensioned correctly.

Weight is 20 grams capped. Balance point sits at the grip without the cap posted. The nib on a medium unit has moderate feedback and lays down a clean, consistent line across most papers. Extra-fine and broad variants exist; the medium is the standard recommendation.

No cartridges. No converter needed. Fill it from a bottle and write.

Alternatives have multiplied in the decades since: Pelikan M200, TWSBI 580, various Chinese pens in the same idiom. None are identical. The Lamy 2000 remains the reference.
