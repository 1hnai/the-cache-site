The Lamy Safari costs thirty pounds and lasts indefinitely. That ratio is difficult to beat in almost any category of object.

It was designed in 1980 by Wolfgang Fabian with a hard-wearing ABS plastic body, a moulded grip section that forces your fingers into the correct position, and a wire clip bent from a single piece of stainless steel. The nibs are available in extra-fine through to bold and are interchangeable with a pull. You can swap nibs, refill with a cartridge or converter, and have the pen professionally serviced — though it rarely needs it.

The Safari works as a daily writer because it isn't precious. It goes into bags without special cases. It survives being dropped. The ink flow is consistent and the nib doesn't scratch. For anyone moving from ballpoints: the experience is appreciably different, not in a ritual way but in a practical one. Your handwriting improves because the pen rewards slower movement.

The colour range rotates slightly each year — a few new additions, a few retirements. The fundamentals don't change. Whatever colour you choose, you're getting the same pen inside.

At this price, there's no reason not to try one.
