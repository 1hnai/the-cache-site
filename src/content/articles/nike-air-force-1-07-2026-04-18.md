There are shoes that exist as fashion objects, rotating in and out of relevance with the seasons. The Air Force 1 is something else. Introduced in 1982 as the first basketball shoe to use Nike Air cushioning, it crossed over to streetwear in the nineties and never looked back. Decades later it remains in constant production — not as nostalgia, but as a staple.

The genius of the design is its restraint. The all-white colourway — white leather upper, white midsole, white outsole — is almost aggressively simple. It works with almost anything. Slim trousers, wide leg denim, shorts in summer. The proportions are just right: not too chunky, not too flat, thick enough to anchor an outfit without overwhelming it.

The construction is solid. Full-grain leather upper, foam midsole that holds up over years of wear, the distinctive pivot circle on the outsole. These aren't dress shoes — they're built to be worn, daily, without ceremony.

What the AF1 does that most sneakers don't is age well. The leather creases and the sole yellows slightly and the shoe becomes yours in a way that fresh white trainers rarely do. A pair that's six months in often looks better than a pair that's six days in.

At £89, they're one of the most defensible purchases in footwear.
