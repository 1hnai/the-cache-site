Grado has been making headphones in Brooklyn since 1953, and the SR80x is the entry point to a range that audiophiles have recommended to each other for decades without qualification. It's the kind of product that makes you reconsider what value means.

Open-back design means the soundstage breathes. You hear width and depth in a recording that closed-back headphones tend to flatten out. The trade-off is that they leak sound - these are for private listening at home, not commuting - but in the right environment, the presentation is unusually natural. Instruments occupy space. Voices have texture.

The driver is a 44mm dynamic design that Grado developed in-house. The result is a warmth in the midrange that works particularly well with acoustic music, guitar, and anything recorded on tape. Electronic music sounds correct too - detailed and tight in the bass without being overemphasised.

The build is simple to the point of being unfashionable: plastic housing, foam ear cushions, a coiled cable. It won't win any comparison against premium-looking closed-backs from Japanese or German manufacturers. But the cushions are replaceable, the cable terminable, and Grado has been servicing its own headphones for generations. There's something to be said for a product designed to be maintained rather than replaced.

At £99, the SR80x is one of the most honest recommendations in home audio. Buy them, break them in over a few weeks, and they'll still be good ten years from now.
