Bowers & Wilkins has been making loudspeakers in West Sussex since 1966. Their approach has always prioritised acoustic accuracy over marketing, which makes their flagship wireless headphones something of a statement. The Px8 is what happens when a serious speaker company decides to make a pair of headphones without compromising.

The drivers are custom-designed 40mm units developed from B&W's loudspeaker engineering. The housing is carbon fibre. The headband and ear cushions are Nappa leather. The construction manages to feel both austere and luxurious - no flashy branding, no bright accents, just considered materials and engineering logic.

Active noise cancellation performs well, and the 30-hour battery is honest rather than optimistic. But what genuinely separates the Px8 is the sound. There's a precision and spaciousness here that most noise-cancelling headphones don't approach - the treble extends cleanly without harshness, the bass is controlled rather than boosted, and complex orchestral passages don't collapse into a blur.

The B&W Music app supports lossless streaming from Tidal and Qobuz, and aptX Adaptive handles high-resolution Bluetooth if your source supports it. Comfort over long sessions is good - the ear cushions are generous and the clamping force is light enough to wear for hours.

At £699, the Px8 is not an impulse purchase. But in a segment full of headphones that prioritise noise cancellation and brand recognition over actual audio quality, it's one of a small handful that sounds like it was designed by people who care deeply about what music sounds like.
