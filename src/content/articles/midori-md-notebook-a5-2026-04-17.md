Most notebooks are vessels. The Midori MD is an argument that the vessel matters. The paper is the point - 72gsm MD paper, developed in-house by Midori, optimised specifically for ink flow and fountain pen compatibility. It's smooth enough that fine-nib pens glide without drag, with enough tooth to keep the line precise. Ink dries fast, bleeds almost never, and the sheet weight resists ghosting from the reverse side.

The cover is ivory cotton paper stock, simple and uncoated - it will scuff and acquire character over time rather than staying plastically pristine. The binding is sewn, which means pages lie flat and the notebook opens without resistance. All of this sounds like a description of features. What it produces in use is a piece of paper that makes you want to write.

Available in ruled, grid, and blank variants. The grid is a 4mm spacing - tight enough to be precise, open enough to not feel constraining. For sketching, planning, or longhand notes, the blank version is the more useful of the three.

At £16 for an A5 it's not cheap compared to the supermarket alternative, but the comparison isn't meaningful. The MD sits in the same category as the Leuchtturm1917 and Moleskine but does something different: less branding, more material honesty. The cover and pages are the same off-white tone. The whole object has a quietness to it.

Worth buying a single one to understand what the fuss is about. Most people buy more after that.
