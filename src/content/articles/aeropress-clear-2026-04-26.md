The original AeroPress has been a fixture in specialty coffee since 2005, and its reputation is entirely earned. It produces a clean, bright cup with low acidity, it's nearly indestructible, and the brewing variables — temperature, grind, steep time, pressure — are flexible enough to reward experimentation without demanding it.

The Clear is the same brewer made from transparent Tritan plastic, which does one useful thing: you can see exactly what's happening as you brew. The bloom, the extraction, the drawdown — all visible. For anyone still learning the mechanics of coffee brewing, that visibility is genuinely helpful rather than merely aesthetic.

The case for the AeroPress over a pour-over or French press comes down to control and speed. Brew time is two to three minutes from boil. Cleanup is a press and a rinse. The paper filters produce a cleaner cup than a metal French press filter, with none of the fine grounds that settle at the bottom. And because the brewing chamber is sealed, you can adjust steep time and temperature independently in ways that an open pour-over won't allow.

At £35 it's one of the best value-per-cup pieces of brewing equipment available. The Clear edition adds nothing to the brewing process — but if you're going to have it on the counter, it's a better-looking object than the original black.
